SECTION 4

So, now we're going to look at some of the reasons why it's beneficial to include drama activities, such as role-play, in a range of subject areas. There's a lot of evidence to show that drama helps a child's personal development.
Firstly, creating the space for children to express themselves freely in a group situation helps to give them confidence in their own opinions and ideas. Drama activities can work especially well for shy or introverted children. [Q31 → confidence]
Secondly, unscripted improvisation activities, where children work from their imagination, give children the opportunity to gain experience in taking risks, and encourage them to say things they might normally be afraid to say. [Q32 → risks]
Drama can also support the child's educational development. For example, the emphasis placed on cooperative activities means that group members must learn the value of listening to one another. [Q33 → listening] Being involved in any sort of performance, which relies on everyone working together and playing their part, also helps children to be responsible not only for their learning, but for their general behaviour. [Q34 → responsible]

Now I'd like to look at how role-play can be a valuable learning tool. The value of role-play for children who are having difficulties, as a means of therapy, is now widely accepted. [Q35 → therapy] Acting out aspects of their own life without explicitly stating it can help children judge their own behaviour objectively.
More generally, role-play also allows the teacher to explore potentially controversial or taboo subjects in a safe setting in which children feel at ease. [Q36 → safe] It's essential children feel they won't be criticized or judged. Getting children to examine their thoughts and opinions carefully can lead to a deeper self-knowledge and a stronger awareness of morality. This is an important factor in developing social skills. [Q37 → morality]

Let's think now about how drama can enrich the learning process for the study of history. Bringing characters from the past to life by doing classroom drama activities is an easy way to improve the level of the children's participation in the lesson. [Q38 → participation] Getting children to re-enact scenes from history helps them to remember what happened and why it was important, as well as helping to bring the past alive. [Q39 → remember]
Another way is to ask children to find solutions to historical problems using role-play. This can help to focus attention on why certain decisions were taken. [Q40 → problems] For example, children can be asked to decide how best to defend their town from attack.
